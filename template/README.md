# Your expertise vault

Pick one thing. In a year, know it better than most adults who work in it, and be able to prove it.

This vault is how. It has two halves, and they belong to different people.

## Two halves

**`library/` is your agent's.** It keeps the facts: what you read, watched and heard, each with where it came from. You can read it, question it and correct it. It writes; you check.

**`mind/` is yours.** Only you write here. Your agent reads it but never touches it. It holds:

- **insights**: something you figured out by connecting two or more things you know, in your own words;
- **positions**: something you believe about your area and would defend, with what would change your mind;
- **practice**: the work that gets you there (below).

Why split it? Because collecting facts feels like learning, and it isn't enough. An expert has opinions they can defend. Your agent can find facts all day. Only you can have a point of view.

## The ladder

```
positions   what I believe and would defend     ← yours
insights    what I figured out                   ← yours
facts       what is known, with sources          ← your agent keeps these
```

The dashboard (`_dashboard.md`) shows how far up you are, topic by topic, and names your next step.

## Five practices

Entrepreneurs at Babson College learn by practicing five things. So will you.

1. **Play.** Explore with no goal. Follow curiosities. Write hypotheses: guess before you look things up, then check. A wrong hypothesis costs nothing and teaches a lot.
2. **Empathy.** Talk to people who live inside your area: the workers, the customers, the people with the problem. Ask about the last time something happened, not what they think of your idea.
3. **Creation.** Make things from what you know: a post, a video, a sketch, a prototype. Making shows you what you don't understand yet.
4. **Experimentation.** Test your hypotheses and positions against reality. The best test is the cheapest one that could prove you wrong.
5. **Reflection.** Once a week, look back. What did you learn? What changed your mind? What do you believe now?

## Your week

- Most days: capture curiosities, read or watch one thing, ask your agent to file it.
- Twice a week: one practice that isn't reading (an interview, a creation, an experiment).
- Once a week: reflection. Then look at the dashboard and pick your next step.

## Start

Open this folder with your agent and say: **"Let's start."** It will help you choose your area by playing, not by filling in a form.
